How to Play Pentomino

Tap, slide, flick! A friendly guide to getting along with your puzzle pieces

Welcome to the World of Pentomino! 🧩

Five little squares. So many possible shapes. And one very important question: where does this piece go?

Pentomino is a puzzle about finding the right place and the right orientation for each piece. Sometimes a piece needs a little turn. Sometimes it needs a flip. And sometimes it seems determined to be difficult. 😆

This guide is here when you need a hand. You don’t have to read it all before playing. Jump to whatever you’re curious about.

Would you rather try it than read about it?

Head over to Let’s Play! — Gesture Practice to practice tapping, dragging, and flicking with a piece. The interactive tutorial is currently in Japanese.

Three Little Moves to Master 🖐️

Just three gestures to get you started!

Tap, draaaag, flick! Master these three moves, and you’re ready to play.

① Tap: Give It a Twirl! 🔄

Tap a piece that hasn’t been placed on the board to rotate it 90° counterclockwise.

Not quite the right angle? Tap again. And again. Round it goes!

② Drag: Take It for a Stroll! 👆

Touch and hold a piece, then drag it slowly to move it around.

  • Red outline: You’ve got hold of the piece!
  • Blue outline: Here’s a spot where it can go!
  • Release your finger: Place the piece there.

Bring the piece near a valid spot on the board, and it’ll snap right into position.

But there’s one little catch!

Blue doesn’t mean you’ve solved it!

It simply means the piece fits in that spot. You can check whether it’s the correct placement later.

③ Flick: Flip It! ↔︎️

Grab a piece, then give it a quick flick and release your finger!

The piece flips like a mirror image.

Flick direction What happens
← Left or → Right Flip horizontally (mirror left to right)
↑ Up or ↓ Down Flip vertically (mirror top to bottom)

Don’t worry! Your piece won’t go flying off the screen. 😄

It flips right where you release it.

Drag or flick? What’s the difference?

It’s all about speed when you release your finger!

Drag slowly to move a piece. Flick quickly to flip it.

If the app keeps mistaking one for the other, try adjusting the gesture sensitivity under the gear icon ⚙️.

The default threshold is 70 mm/second.

Tap to rotate. Drag to move. Flick to flip!

Three little moves. A whole world of puzzles! 🧩

Choose Your Belt 🥋

Pentomino has three difficulty modes. No need to earn a black belt on your first day!

⚪ White Belt: First Steps

Practice picking up, moving, and placing pieces.

The pieces are presented in placement order, with their orientations already set correctly. You can concentrate on getting comfortable with the controls.

Best place to start if you’re new.

🟡 Yellow Belt: Time to Twist and Flip

Practice rotating and flipping pieces into position.

The pieces still appear in placement order, but this time their orientations aren’t prepared for you. You’ll need your tap and flick skills!

⚫ Black Belt: The Real Challenge

Figure out the solution for yourself.

The pieces appear in alphabetical order, and their orientations aren’t preset. You’ll decide which piece goes where — and how it should face.

You can change belts whenever you like

Your progress is saved separately for each belt. Switch modes to suit your mood without losing your place in the others.

A Little Help When You’re Stuck

Check: Did I Get It Right? 🔎

Tap Check to see whether your placed pieces are correct.

There’s no point deduction if everything you’ve placed is correct. Incorrect placements, however, may cost points.

Hint: Just One Little Clue! 💡

Sometimes the pieces simply refuse to cooperate. That’s what Hint is for.

When you request a hint, the app first checks the board and removes incorrectly placed pieces. Then it places one remaining piece in its correct position, working backward from the end of the solution’s placement order.

The point deduction covers the number of incorrectly placed pieces plus one hint. Helpful? Absolutely. Free? Not quite! 😉

Pick a Puzzle: 2,339 to Explore!

There are 10 levels, L1 through L10, with 2,339 puzzles altogether. That’s enough to keep even a dedicated puzzle fan busy for a while.

Use the ← and → buttons to move between puzzles:

  • Tap: move one puzzle at a time.
  • Hold for at least 1 second: move by 10 puzzles, roughly every 0.3 seconds.
  • Keep holding beyond 3 seconds: move by 100 puzzles, roughly every 0.3 seconds.

Puzzle numbers stay within the range 1–2,339.

What’s Behind the Gear Icon? ⚙️

Tap the gear icon at the top of the screen to open Settings. You can also press and hold the puzzle title.

Language: Choose from Japanese, English, Spanish, French, German, Korean, Simplified Chinese, or Traditional Chinese. The instructions change to match your selection.

Completion sound: Turn the sound played when you finish a puzzle on or off.

Placement feedback: Depending on your device, the app uses either vibration or sound to signal a placement — not both at once.

Difficulty and history: Progress is stored separately for each belt. Clear History removes the record for the currently selected belt only. Make sure that’s what you want before clearing it.

Sensitivity: Adjust the threshold that separates a swipe from a flick, along with the duration or strength of placement feedback.

A Peek Behind the Puzzle 🧠

There’s a reason the pieces arrive in a particular order in White and Yellow Belt modes.

When the app constructs a solution, it fills available spaces beginning at the top left, moving through a column before proceeding to the right. The beginner modes follow that placement order to help you learn.

So yes, there is a method to the madness. Your puzzle pieces aren’t entirely making it up as they go!

Ready to Give It a Go?

If your first attempt looks like a pile of misplaced shapes, you’re in good company.

Remember the three moves: tap to rotate, swipe to move, flick to flip.

Then experiment. Try another angle. Ask for a hint if you need one. Most importantly, have fun discovering where those five-square pieces belong. 🧩

About this guide

This is a friendlier English adaptation of the current Japanese app instructions. Features, controls, and scoring details may change between app versions. The interactive gesture tutorial linked above is a separate prototype and may not perfectly match the app’s gesture sensitivity.
